# Offering Rules

Apply before customer-facing pages or initial pricing, during launch preparation, and whenever the offer changes, including after launch. Keep copy brief and understandable to the intended audience.

- Explain the audience, problem, supported benefits, and next action. Use verified capabilities and current screenshots; distinguish available features from plans. Pre-launch pages must accurately identify waitlists, previews, and availability.
- Substantiate measurable claims, comparisons, testimonials, and trust marks. Never invent customers, guarantees, savings, scarcity, or certifications. Commitments and disclosures follow [legal.md](legal.md); verify data/security claims under [security.md](security.md#security-review). Presentation follows [project design guide](../design/README.md). Verify links and advertised signup/purchase journeys, including mobile.

Use the `pricing-research` skill recorded in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) before initial pricing or material commercial changes. Compare relevant products, customer value, delivery/support costs, and applicable constraints. Competitor prices are evidence, not a target or proof of an optimal price. Assume no subscription tiers, discounts, or universal refund window.

- Maintain one approved commercial record indexed from private `docs/internal/` under [documentation.md](documentation.md): audience, offering, rationale, effective version, and approval. Link canonical billing configuration and approved policies instead of duplicating them. Separate proposals from active terms.
- The owner or explicitly authorized business delegate approves initial prices and material changes; reuse recorded decisions. Research does not authorize live billing changes. Record effects on existing customers, preserving commitments and required notices under `standards/legal.md`.
- Research refund eligibility, deadlines, renewals, consumed services, and cancellation separately. Policy wording, applicability review, and commitments follow `standards/legal.md`; approved public policies belong in `docs/public/legal/`.
- Keep landing pages, feature/plan comparisons, pricing pages, checkout, billing, and customer docs consistent with approved amounts, intervals, currencies, limits, charges, trials, renewals, cancellation, and refunds. Update affected drafts before issue completion; activate with the corresponding authorized offering under [launch.md](launch.md). Free products need only applicable rules.

## Search visibility

For public-facing websites, apply these checks during planning, page implementation and relevant releases. Private application areas and development previews are not intended for indexing. Preserve existing URLs, visibility and production behavior during adoption.

- Plan each important page around its audience, purpose and likely search intent. Use useful, accurate content and descriptive page titles, headings and descriptions. Do not invent claims, reviews or other facts, stuff keywords, or rely on a meta keywords tag for Google.
- Check rendered pages, public links, mobile usability, crawl and indexing controls, canonical signals and sitemaps as applicable. Inspect existing theme, CMS and SEO-plugin output first; reuse its settings and schema instead of adding conflicting or duplicate output. Keep private material out of search and external test tools.
- Use relevant structured data that matches visible, current content; prefer JSON-LD where appropriate. Check the selected type against Google's current [structured data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) and [supported features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery). Validate representative rendered markup and, for eligible public pages, use the [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results); record unsupported or unavailable checks. Syntactic validity does not guarantee a rich result or ranking.
- Follow [Google's AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide): useful, accessible content and ordinary search fundamentals come first. Special AI schema or `llms.txt` is not required for Google; treat other AI-specific recommendations as hypotheses, not guarantees. Choose search and training crawler access separately under the project's privacy choices. Use Search Console when available; do not infer actual indexing, rankings or AI citations from local tests.

For public sites, install the [Marketing Skills](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills) `seo-audit` and `schema` skills project-scoped using the active agent's supported discovery. Include each complete skill and its references, preserve the upstream [MIT license and notice](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/LICENSE), verify discovery and readable references, and record source, revision and paths in `AGENTS.md`. Use them for relevant audit, implementation and review; project facts and current official search guidance take precedence over skill suggestions. Reuse existing approved marketing context without creating a competing source. Use `ai-seo` optionally for a scoped AI-search task under the same installation and evidence rules. Maintain installed skills under [updates.md](updates.md); installation does not authorize publication, paid services or changes to live sites.

For other Corey Haines marketing skills, explain the relevant options when substantive marketing begins and request approval from the owner or a human developer explicitly authorized to approve skill installation. Record the decision; honor it without repeated prompts. Deferral does not block work. Before installation, review skills, dependencies, license, and project fit; record revision and location.
